# Setline project brief

## Product and stage

Setline helps experienced lifters follow their own routines, record performance, and review exercise-specific progress. Workouts are always authored by the lifter. Stage 14 adds optional account backups, offline export and restore, and selective sharing. **View sample data** opens the separately stored sample week anchored on November 17, 2026.

- **Today / Log** shows the training week or a rolling seven-day window. It distinguishes planned, active, completed, skipped, missed, and moved sessions. It also offers daily bodyweight, optional readiness, front/back muscle maps, and an expandable workout score proposal.
- **Routines** creates, edits, duplicates, categorizes, and schedules manually authored plans. It shows the current plan and its saved version history, and opens the Exercise Library.
- **History / Progress** provides a calendar and chronological list, session details, filters, and side-by-side comparisons. There are separate comparisons for sessions and photos. Progress shows weekly recaps, exercise analytics, progression review, forecast accuracy, and bodyweight history. Calendar details show saved forecasts, photo links, calorie inputs for finished logged workouts, and historical weigh-in editing.
- **Settings** provides system, light, and dark appearance, solid navigation, and kilogram or pound display.
- The workout bar resumes the persisted active session across all four tabs. The logger shows planned targets and prior results, saves field edits and notes, and supports one-tap completion and undo. Set and exercise controls change order, add and remove rows, or skip pending work. The busy-equipment queue preserves rows for later in the session.
- The workout toolbar shows the next pending set, provides thumb-reachable completion and timing controls, and supports one-step undo of completion, deletion, skip, and reorder. Possible entry typos appear as advisory feedback alongside the set.

## Architecture and data conventions

- The iOS 17+ app uses SwiftUI and a single main-actor repository. Each successful record write atomically replaces the versioned `TrainingData.json` file in Application Support. Active sessions and their sets save incrementally. Unsupported schemas block user-store writes. `AppStorage` holds display preferences.
- Schema 2 migrates manually entered bodyweight from `TrainingState-v1.json` into dated records. The old file stays available and its modification time supplies missing timestamps. Legacy active sample workouts resume in the isolated `SamplePreview-v1.json` state. Bundled sample routines, history, and new sample entries stay outside the personal repository.
- Navigation uses a 50% surface tint over a blur. The app and browser honor the platform reduced-transparency preference as well as the in-app setting. Native layouts use Dynamic Type and accessibility labels.
- Both implementations work without an internet connection or an account. The browser preview serves its assets over loopback and stores routine plans, schedules, session logs, bodyweight, preferences, and the sample preview in local keys. [Sample data](ios/Setline/Resources/SampleData.json)
- The layout draws on daily dashboard and progress patterns, as well as Apple’s native control and material conventions. [Appetite UI Fitness](https://www.appetiteui.com/docs/screens-fitness) · [Apple Design Resources](https://developer.apple.com/design/resources/) · [Apple’s Reduce Transparency documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityreducetransparency)

## Training records

- Native records have UUIDs and timestamps. Workout dates use Gregorian `YYYY-MM-DD` keys. A session captures the device’s local date and time zone when started. Bodyweight edits retain one ID per day.
- The repository appends a routine version when its name or prescriptions change. A scheduled workout points to a specific routine version. Starting a session copies that version, exercises, equipment, and prescriptions into the session. Performed sets reference the copied prescription IDs. Performance forecasts store ranges and evidence separately from prescriptions and progression suggestions.
- Equipment has its own type and ID. Comparison keys use the exercise ID, equipment ID, measurement, load basis, and rep convention. Different cable or machine records stay distinct even when their labels or settings match.
- Physical mass is stored in kilograms. One pound is exactly `0.45359237 kg`. Combined weight is the total loaded mass, including the bar for barbell lifts. A pair of 20 kg dumbbells is recorded as either 20 kg per dumbbell or 40 kg combined, with the basis stored explicitly. The unit preference changes display only.
- Rep counts are total, equal per side, or separate left and right counts. Per-side targets apply to each side. Added load is the external weight applied to a bodyweight movement. Assistance is a positive amount that offsets load. Each has its own measurement type.
- Machine settings are device-specific labels such as `007`. They retain their exact text and stay outside mass conversion. Duration and rest use seconds, distance uses meters, and RIR counts reps in reserve.
- Weekday assignments pin routine versions. The week start changes the calendar layout while a rotation uses its own calendar-day cadence. A rotation shows one upcoming step. Finishing its scheduled session with at least one recorded set or explicitly skipping the step advances it. A missed day or an empty finished session leaves the same step next. A move holds the step until the new date, and past rotation misses stay visible.
- Calendar moves preserve the original date and the referenced routine version. A move can place a program session outside its original week while retaining its program and week details. Completed workouts retain their own actual local start date.
- Program blocks group pinned versions within inclusive local dates. Each week can be marked training, progression, or deload and store the lifter’s notes. The lifter chooses or creates each routine version with the intended targets. Program entries can coexist with recurring assignments.
- Readiness is one optional record per local date with 1–5 soreness, sleep quality, energy, and motivation ratings. Ratings never alter workout targets. Existing sleep-minute values remain available in storage.
- The recent-training map uses local calendar days since the most recent session with an actual set for an exercise involving each muscle. Primary and secondary involvement both count. The date comes from the session record, and unfinished sessions contribute only sets already logged.
- The offline exercise catalog uses stable IDs and original instruction copy. Favorites and exclusions are stored by exercise ID. Exclusions hide exercises from normal browsing and substitutions, with a separate collection for review. Substitutions use shared muscles and user-selected equipment types.
- Custom exercise names, muscles, cues, and setup notes are editable. A different equipment type or variation label requires a new exercise ID. Previously saved routine versions and workout snapshots retain their recorded exercise data. Physical equipment IDs still distinguish individual stations in performance records.
- Demonstrations support bundled images and video. Entries without a supplied asset display an explicit placeholder. [Exercise catalog](ios/Setline/Resources/ExerciseCatalog.json)
- Routine versions store ordered exercise slots, a category, and optional groups. A superset contains two consecutive exercises, and a circuit can contain two or more. Round count and rest between exercises and between rounds are recorded separately. Duplicates get fresh routine, group, and slot IDs.
- Set blocks separate warm-up and working prescriptions. Set structure, AMRAP, and the effort goal are independent. A goal can use RIR, RPE, or failure. Recorded effort outcomes are stored on performed sets separately from the prescribed goal.
- Workout-only edits save an effective plan on the active session. The starting snapshot and source routine version are retained. History corrections update performed sets, notes, and the incomplete flag while preserving the session identity, original prescription, and recorded clock. Repeats reference the corrected session and start with an empty performed-set list.
- Pending, skipped, and completed are separate set states. Prefills and edits save as inputs. Explicit completion creates a performed result with typed measurements and optional actual RIR, RPE, and failure. End early retains completed, skipped, and unfinished rows as recorded. Repeats reset statuses, notes, and the queue while copying the session’s exercise and set structure.
- Today-only edits are the default. **Update routine** explicitly promotes the current order, set rows, and edited targets to a new version of the linked routine. Older versions, recorded effort, session notes, and completed sessions retain their original data. A newer routine version requires editing from Routines.
- Rest starts on explicit completion. Ungrouped sets use their prescribed rest or 90 seconds when unset. Supersets and circuits use the configured interval before the next available set in the same round or the next round. Warm-ups and transitions out of a group use ordinary set rest. A zero interval is ready immediately.
- Elapsed time comes from the session start and finish timestamps. Active time excludes explicit pauses. Countdown timestamps and pause state persist across interruptions. Optional measured rest counts active seconds between completion and the next completion or **Skip rest**, including time after the countdown reaches zero. Extension and restart only change the countdown. An edited rest value is saved alongside the measured seconds, with both labeled. Finishing clears the unclosed final interval.
- Equipment profiles store a bar weight and available matching plate pairs, or a user-entered weight increment in its configured unit. The plate calculator finds balanced setups and attainable neighboring totals. Per-side prescriptions can log explicit left and right reps and an optional different right-side load. Timed and distance sets also support separate left and right values. Both sides’ results remain separate from the prescription.
- Manual history entries use a specified local start and optional duration. A saved routine version can supply the prescription. Only sets explicitly marked performed with valid actuals count. Incomplete cards include in-progress work, early finishes, zero-set finishes, and records with skipped or unfinished rows.
- Derived history uses performed sets. Volume sums load × reps once per eligible set. A bilateral set recorded per dumbbell counts both dumbbells. Separate sides keep each load paired with that side’s reps. Assistance, settings, and unloaded sets stay out of volume. Identified machines have their own series. Unidentified stations stay scoped to the logged slot and session, and workout-wide volumes exclude machine and cable values. Comparison deltas for reps and volume use matching variants. A finish summary prefers an earlier finished version of the same routine, then the most recent matching performed exercise variant.
- Exercise charts use finished sessions and group observations by local date. Load and estimated 1RM show the daily peak. Reps, volume, duration, and distance show daily totals. Warm-up charts and records are separate. The estimate uses Epley for bilateral free-weight working sets of 2–10 reps and shows the recorded load for a single. It assumes a hard set and varies with effort, technique, and exercise. [1RM validation study](https://efyc.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/article/download/efyce222/15809?inline=1)
- Weekly analytics use the chosen training-week start. Scheduled plans count on their scheduled date and performed sets count on their actual session date. Unscheduled sessions use the original snapshot for planned sets. Direct and indirect muscle sets are counted separately from the saved primary and secondary roles, with primary taking precedence for the same set and muscle.
- Plateau flags default to less than 2% improvement in estimated 1RM over four eligible sessions. The rule compares the first observation with the best later observation in the window. Deload observations are excluded. Program, routine-version, and equipment changes or training gaps reset the window. Users can change the metric, sample count, improvement threshold, and gap length or turn flags off. Chart annotations show recorded deloads and changes in training context.
- Corrected results update summaries, charts, and upcoming forecasts on the next read. The legacy prediction cache keeps its invalidation behavior. Issued forecast revisions and their frozen previous-session benchmarks are stored separately on their workout and survive history corrections.
- The bodyweight trend is a trailing seven-calendar-day mean of recorded readings, including the day shown. It requires at least two readings in that window, skips missing days, and draws points only on days with readings. Editing a past reading recomputes the affected means. The optional signed target rate is stored in kilograms per week and displayed in the chosen unit. A weigh-in is optional throughout workout logging.
- Progress photo links belong to a finished workout or directly to a local calendar date. A workout link appears on the session’s recorded local date. The iPhone app stores Photos asset IDs and reads existing app-relative references. It uses PhotosPicker for selection and saves new camera photos to Photos. Previews require device permission and a locally available asset. Unlinking removes the reference only. Photos library sync remains under the user’s separate device settings. App exports and sharing exclude photo content. [Apple’s iCloud Photos documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108782)
- The browser preview stores file handles in IndexedDB where the local file picker is supported. Preview URLs use in-memory data from the selected files. Native camera capture and Photos library permission controls are available in the iPhone app.
- Progression rules are opt-in and keyed to exercise, equipment, measurement, and loading and rep conventions. Name a cable or machine station to make it comparable. Physical increments use canonical kilograms, including per-dumbbell values when prescribed. Timed and distance increments use seconds and meters. An increment comes from the lifter’s available equipment. Machine settings remain exact labels and support reps progression only.
- The rule reads the latest comparable finished workout and requires the current straight working prescription. Warm-ups, AMRAP, drop, and rest-pause sets stay separate. Both sides of unilateral results must meet their own requirements. A planned deload or unfinished prescribed straight sets hold the target. Load and assistance use the top of each rep range by default, with a minimum-rep option. A recorded miss can hold or step back affected blocks. Effort checks use RIR or RPE only when every eligible set recorded that metric.
- Suggestions are derived from saved results on review. A history correction or completed workout refreshes the evidence. A suggestion shows its source date, the prescriptions and actuals, and its reasoning. Acceptance rechecks the evidence and saves a new routine version. Manual overrides use the routine editor. Existing session snapshots and pinned calendar versions keep their original prescriptions.
- Forecasts describe the first completed straight working set for an existing exercise. Each slot receives a snapshot on workout start or when it is added. Changes to its setup or prescription before any recorded result can append a revision. The snapshots carry a timestamp, model version, source session and set IDs, ranges, and a frozen benchmark. Manual backfills and corrections record when the evidence became available. Forecasts only use finished sessions and results available at issue time. Pre-existing history can inform live forecasts. Scoring starts with the saved prospective forecasts.
- `recent-median-v1` needs three sessions within the prior 120 days and uses up to eight. It separates equipment, tracking, load and rep conventions, and exact machine settings. With enough matches, it narrows evidence by first-or-later exercise order, planned rep range or load, similar prescribed rest, observed rest after warm-ups, and recorded effort near the training goal. Unilateral results use the lower reps, load, duration, or distance, and the higher assistance. Load and reps are summarized separately.
- Warm-ups, special sets, and planned deloads stay outside training outcomes. In samples of five or more, the baseline can drop isolated results beyond the outer quartile fences, with minimum tolerances of 0.5 kg, one rep, two seconds, or two meters. The median is the typical value. Bands extend to cover the 10th and 90th percentiles, or the full observed span for samples below five. Minimum band widths avoid a point estimate appearing exact. The band widens by 50% after 21 days since the last result used. Confidence labels describe the amount and similarity of evidence. The ranges have no calibrated coverage guarantee.
- Forecast accuracy compares the mean absolute error and observed range coverage on prospective saved forecasts. The previous-session benchmark repeats the latest earlier comparable result available at issue time. Scores require the same equipment, tracking, machine setting, and first-or-later order. No trained model, bodyweight, readiness, or network service is used. Any later model will need sufficient historical and prospective evidence to justify adoption.
- Weekly recaps use the configured week start. The scheduled denominator includes pending, skipped, and missed sessions and counts a moved session at its destination only. The completed count requires a finished, complete log linked to a calendar slot. Unscheduled completions and incomplete logs are shown separately. Logged sets from incomplete sessions and deloads contribute to volume and muscle counts. Warm-up and working coverage keep direct and indirect roles separate.
- The recap shows new records against earlier comparable working history. Session changes compare first straight working sets with the same setup, rep and loading conventions, exact machine setting, and first-or-later exercise order. Deloads stay outside those comparisons. The bodyweight view uses actual readings and the trailing seven-day mean. The upcoming workout comes from today’s schedule and shows the pinned targets and live forecast separately, even when browsing a past recap.
- The daily score policy receives the selected date, session records, and schedule. The production policy always returns unavailable. The expandable proposals cover intended work, planned targets and optional effort, deload context, and evidence quality and safety. The candidate rules give no bonus for extra sets, extra load, or training to failure and allow safety-related early stops without penalty. A scoring formula remains a separate product decision.
- Calories are optional gross estimates using the 2024 Adult Compendium resistance-training category selected by the lifter and `MET × kilograms × activity hours`. Minutes are explicitly entered and bodyweight comes from a reading on the workout’s local date. The formula approximates one MET as one kcal per kilogram per hour and includes resting expenditure during that interval. Missing inputs produce unavailable. Weekly totals identify how many finished logged workouts contributed. [Compendium activity codes](https://pacompendium.com/conditioning-exercise/) · [Adult Compendium method](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10818145/)

- Full backup envelopes use `setline-backup` version 1 with a client identifier. The profile includes record identity, snapshots, relationships, active work, and settings. Restore validates identifiers, types, and relationships, then replaces the local profile after confirmation. Sample data stays separate. iPhone and browser backups use their respective record schemas. CSV and the `setline-routine` version 1 format work across clients. Routine import creates new slot, group, and routine IDs and detects equivalent plans.
- Backup controls cover notes, bodyweight and its target, readiness, forecasts, and photo references. Photo files are excluded. Optional references can be unavailable on another device. The browser preserves existing permissioned handles for matching same-profile references and restores other links as metadata. Workout summaries include only previewed text. Notes, bodyweight, and effort are off by default for shared summaries. Bodyweight CSV is separate from workout CSV. CSV columns carry explicit units and neutralize spreadsheet formulas entered as text.
- Optional accounts use the self-hosted loopback service with an HTTPS reverse proxy for remote use. Passwords use salted scrypt, bearer tokens are hashed on the server, and account backups use authenticated encryption in SQLite. Clients keep the token in memory. One manually saved backup per client and account can be read, replaced, or removed. Local file export, import, and the training workflow work while signed out. No remote deployment or password recovery is included.
